# The Far Side of the Horizon — in preparation

**What a CPT fold does at a black hole, and what it does not**

Companion to *Separate Ways and the Upside Down*, the paper this repository's root holds.

## Status

This paper is **not finished**. As it stands it is missing its introduction and methods
sections (it currently opens mid-argument and its section numbering starts at "3"), and it
has no closing material: no discussion, no acknowledgements, no references list. Treat
`far_side_of_the_horizon.md` as a working draft, not a citable reference, until this note
is removed.

## What the main paper does and does not take from it

The main paper cites this companion 25 times. Every citation that touches the main paper's
headline results — the dark-matter mass ceiling, the neutrino line, the neutrino-mass sum —
carries an explicit disclaimer that the result does not depend on it (see §1.1: *"None of
the results below depend on any of it: the bound, the neutrino line and the mass floor
stand or fall on §§2 and 3 alone"*, repeated in §2's opening). Everything this companion
actually supplies concerns black-hole horizon behaviour specifically: which one-copy
readings of the far side of a horizon fail and why, the seam coefficient the fold leaves
open, the two-sided black-hole class as a candidate growth channel for JWST's early
black holes, and a ringdown test that — by the main paper's own account — waits on a
measurement precision that does not exist yet.
